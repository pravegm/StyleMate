# StyleMate

An iOS app that turns your camera roll into an organized digital wardrobe and styles you every day — **entirely on your iPhone**. No photo, face or wardrobe data is sent to any AI service.

StyleMate finds the photos of *you* in your library, cuts out every garment and accessory you're wearing, names it (category, product, colours, pattern, material, fit…), de-duplicates it against what you already own, and files it into your closet. When you want to get dressed, it builds five outfits for today from your own clothes — tuned to the weather, the occasion, your mood and what you've worn recently — and lets you log the look on a calendar.

## How it works

### 1. Finding you in your photos (on-device face matching)

- During onboarding you take a **three-frame selfie** (straight on, then a slight turn each way; `SelfieCameraService`). Each frame is checked on-device (one face, big enough, centred, lit, no sunglasses) before it's accepted.
- Then **Find yourself** (`Services/Identity/`, `FindYourselfView`) looks through up to ~1,500 recent photos, embeds every usable face, groups faces of the same person (greedy centroid clustering), ranks the groups by similarity to your selfie and asks "Is this you?" with one tap to confirm. The confirmed faces become your reference **gallery**, so the scan recognises you across angles, lighting and years.
- Faces are detected and 5-point-landmarked by the bundled **YuNet** detector (OpenCV Zoo, MIT; Core ML), aligned to the canonical ArcFace 112×112 template, and embedded with **SFace** (OpenCV Zoo, Apache-2.0; 128-d, flip-averaged). Large library photos get an extra pass over four overlapping tiles, so distant faces aren't missed. Every similarity bar lives in one profile (`FaceModelProfile`), calibrated on LFW; embeddings are tagged with the model version so a model change rebuilds the gallery. Both models are commercially usable; the earlier InsightFace SCRFD + AdaFace pair (non-commercial) is no longer built or shipped (`docs/MODEL-LICENSING.md`).
- Each library photo is matched by **cosine similarity** against the gallery with tiered confidence and a runner-up margin. Auto-adding needs the gallery's consensus (the mean of the two best-matching gallery faces), so one odd gallery entry can never pull a stranger in; review needs one good match:
  - **confident** → analysed straight away (in a multi-person photo only *your* body is isolated via a person-instance mask, so other people's clothes are never extracted); very confident matches that also match your **selfie** (and clearly beat anyone else in the shot) **teach the gallery** a new angle of your face — learned faces are capped at half the gallery and never displace the selfie or faces you confirmed;
  - **probably you** → queued for an **"Is this you?"** review (home banner → `FaceReviewView`); confirming teaches the gallery and extracts those outfits;
  - anyone else → skipped.
- The scan (`PhotoScanService` + `Services/Scan/`) starts with the **last 12 months**, with items appearing live ("All photos" is one tap away). The very first scan begins with a **"First 40" pass** (`FirstLook`): it ranks the last 90 days by Vision full-body person detection on small thumbnails (one person, whole body in view, a face to recognise; screenshots, documents and scenery drop out) and extracts the best ~40 first, so the closet fills within minutes; then it continues newest first. It skips screenshots, tiny images and panoramas, keeps only the best frame of a burst / same-moment group, pipelines loading and identity ahead of extraction, and paces itself by thermal state and Low Power Mode.
- It downloads iCloud-only originals when needed ("Optimize iPhone Storage"), retries photos that failed to load, and checkpoints progress to `Application Support/ScanProgress/` so it resumes exactly. It keeps going briefly when you leave the app, continues **overnight while charging** (`BGProcessingTask`), and picks up **new photos automatically** when you come back (PhotoKit persistent change history).

### 2. Building the wardrobe (on-device garment extraction — `Services/Wardrobe/`)

For each photo of you, `WardrobeExtractor`:

1. **Finds and crops the person** at a 2:3 portrait aspect so the garments fill the analysis grid (when the person already fills the photo, that first pass is reused instead of run again).
2. **Segments** the crop with **GarmentNet** (`GarmentNet.mlpackage`), StyleMate's own Core ML model: a frozen **DINOv2 ViT-S/14** backbone (Meta, Apache-2.0) plus a small head StyleMate trained from scratch on **Fashionpedia** garments and **COCO** people (annotations CC BY 4.0; `.github/workflows/garment-model.yml`, CPU runners). On a 384×576 grid it labels every pixel with a Fashionpedia garment class (shirt, top, sweater, cardigan, jacket, vest, coat, dress, jumpsuit, pants, shorts, skirt, tights, shoe, bag, hat, headband / headscarf, glasses, watch, belt, scarf, tie…), "body" (skin or hair, so skin never leaks into a garment) or the scene, and gives low-res class probabilities and hood / collar / lapel / sleeve part maps.
   - `GarmentSegmenter` groups the pixels by region and votes inside each, which splits an open coat or jacket from the top underneath it (only when the outer layer flanks the inner one) and a black top from black jeans read as one "jumpsuit"; slivers of a main garment (a coat hem read as "skirt") join their neighbour; the part maps confirm hoods (no "hoodie" without one) and tailored lapels.
   - Grey padding past the photo's edge is masked out, so it never becomes garment. **Close-ups run only where needed**: a found sunglasses / watch / cap / belt too small to cut out cleanly, or a missing item the full-body probabilities still point at (glasses or a hat in the head, a bag, a watch, shoes at the feet); overlapping close-ups share one pass.
3. **Cuts each item out** at full resolution (`GarmentCutout`): edge-aware mask upsampling guided by the photo, colour decontamination of soft edges (no halos), and a pyramid fill of small holes where a hand, arm, hair, strap or belt covered the fabric. Every cutout is trimmed and centred on a transparent square, and shown on a soft studio backdrop (`ItemCutoutTile`).
4. **Names it** (`FashionClassifier`): **Marqo-FashionSigLIP** (Apache-2.0) embeds the cutout and is compared zero-shot against precomputed text embeddings (`FashionVocabulary.json`) for product (and therefore category), pattern, material, fit, sleeve, neckline and length. Tops, layers and one-pieces are ranked across all three groups (a back-view coat the segmenter called a dress becomes a coat); a top seen only between jacket halves or cut off by the frame is limited to everyday tops; rare prints need a clear margin over "Solid". Colours are *measured*: k-means (k=5, merged shades) over the fabric pixels in CIELAB after a damped white balance / exposure correction from the scene, named by hue family then lightness and chroma (so denim is Blue and a dusty-pink coat Pink, not Charcoal and Gray). White vs light grey can't be told from lightness outdoors (a light-grey coat's sunlit side is as bright as a white shirt in shade), so a near-neutral White or Cream main colour is checked against FashionSigLIP zero-shot ("white / bright white / ivory" vs "light grey / silver grey / heather grey", the `neutral_tone` vocabulary group), which sees the heather texture and the contrast against a white backdrop; a garment it reads as grey is named Silver. On Apple Intelligence devices (iOS 27+), Apple's **on-device** foundation model (`SystemLanguageModel`, never Private Cloud Compute) adds a short details line and any clearly readable brand (`GarmentDescriber`).
5. **Scores the view** (size, sharpness, not cut off by the frame, little occlusion). Items too small or too poor to make a good wardrobe image are skipped — a better photo will catch them.
6. **Trust before volume** (`ItemTrust`): the scan only adds items it's sure about. Cutouts that split into several pieces (hair + lens "ties"), views with quality under 0.4, and small jewelry / ties / belts without a clear view are held back — Home shows "We weren't sure about 1 item — tap to add" — and start deselected in the manual review screen.

6. **Studio image** (`Services/Wardrobe/Studio/`): every item gets a lifelike catalog photo made **on the iPhone** by a Stable Diffusion 1.5-class model (dreamshaper-8, Core ML on the Neural Engine). Items are *generated* by image-to-image from a **product template filled with the item's own fabric** (`StudioTemplate`), which locks one whole item, centred, in the catalogue pose, in its real colour and texture: tops, layers and dresses as a flat lay (t-shirt, shirt, jacket, coat, tunic, dress or tank; 0.65); **trousers** front view, full length, with waistband and fly (straight, slim, wide, joggers or leggings by product and fit; 0.65) — every pair, so bent knees, a coat hem across the hips or boots over the ankles don't carry into the render; **shoes** as ONE shoe from the outer side, toe left (sneaker, boot, Chelsea / ankle boot, heel, sandal), upper in the shoe's colour and sole in its own sole colour, asked for plain and unbranded (0.70); **hats** in three-quarter view (wide brim, beanie, bucket; 0.65). All templates share one light from above, one soft contact shadow and one framing, so the closet reads as one catalogue. Text-to-image is only the fallback; without a template the model tends to draw a person wearing the garment. Items with no template are *refined* from the cutout: bags, eyewear, belts, jewelry, skirts, shorts, turbans, and also low shoes (loafers, oxfords, flats, boat shoes, including a "boot" the encoder sees as a low shoe) and caps, whose templates in CI produced pairs of shoes and invented crests. They are refined (image-to-image at ~0.45–0.5), which keeps their exact look. Several candidates are rendered in one batch and cut out of their white background. A candidate is rejected outright for a second object (a second shoe included; for shoes also a zero-shot "pair of shoes" check, since an overlapping pair is one object), an invented brand mark on a shoe or hat (zero-shot logo vs plain), a product shot whose colour drifted from the item, a white panel, a body or mannequin, touching the frame, a "failed render" look (zero-shot), or too little FashionSigLIP similarity to your item (0.80 for a refine, 0.70 text-to-image, 0.55 template). The best of the rest wins; if none is left, the clean cutout stays. Renders run in the background (`StudioQueue`), always from the original cutout, and wait while a photo scan runs, the phone is hot, in Low Power Mode, low on battery or memory; they stop when the app is backgrounded. The item page can flip between **Studio** and **From your photo**. Timing: the CI simulator runs the UNet on its CPU (about 2–4 min per template candidate; 4–8 min per item for two in the studio job); on an iPhone's Neural Engine expect roughly a second per denoising step — 13–14 steps per template candidate, 3 candidates, so about 50 s per item (an estimate from Apple's ml-stable-diffusion benchmarks for SD 1.5, not yet measured on a device). The closet keeps showing the cutout until the render lands. CI's `studio` job renders a fixed set of shoe, hat, trouser and light-grey-coat cutouts every run (`StudioCoverageTests`, `StyleMateTests/Fixtures/studio/`).

Then `DuplicateDetector` compares the item with the wardrobe using the visual embeddings (`ItemEmbeddingStore`) plus attribute gates. The same garment seen again in a **better** photo **upgrades** the wardrobe image instead of adding a duplicate.

There are three ways to add items, all through the same pipeline: **Auto-scan** of a date range, **Photo picker** (up to 15 photos, with "which one is you?" when several people are in a shot), and **Camera**. Picked photos are downsampled to 2048 px, and every extraction (manual or scan) goes through one shared gate (`ExtractionGate`, 1–2 at a time) so memory stays bounded.

### 3. "What should I wear today?" (on-device stylist — `Services/Outfits/`)

`OutfitEngine` builds **five distinct outfits** from your wardrobe:

- **Inputs**: today's weather (met.no, Open-Meteo fallback; city via `CoreLocation`), the occasion (`OutfitType`) or free text ("dinner date, nothing black"), your **mood** (`OutfitMood`: confident, cozy, relaxed, playful, polished, bold, minimal, romantic), your saved style preferences, and your **recent wear history** from the outfit calendar.
- **Rules + scoring**: slot-based construction (top + bottom or a one-piece, optional mid-layer and outerwear, shoes, 0–2 accessories), temperature bands and rain rules, formality fit per occasion, colour harmony on a colour wheel (neutral base + accents, tonal, analogous, complementary; at most one bold pattern), mood and preference fit, classic do-not-pairs, a recency penalty and a learned-taste term (below). A diversity pass keeps the five looks genuinely different.
- **It learns your taste, on the iPhone** (`TasteProfile`, `TasteStore`): "Wear today" teaches the look's pieces, its colour pairings and how dressy you went for that occasion (an outfit logged by hand in My Outfits, or a "Wore this on Tue?" you confirm, teaches the pieces and pairings too); Keep (lock) teaches the piece; Swap marks the swapped-out piece gently; Skip is a weak signal, with an optional "Not my style / Not today" right after it ("Not my style" teaches properly, "Not today" teaches nothing). The model is a few KB per local profile in UserDefaults: every value is capped, decays with a 45-day half-life and is pruned, it is never synced or sent anywhere, and **Profile → Your style → Reset style learning** clears it. In the scorer it is one bounded term (±2) next to the recency penalty, and it may only *promote* a look whose taste-free score is already within 1.2 of the best for option 1 (2.0 for options 2–5, so a habit the rules rate a little lower still makes the deck), so learning reorders good looks but never puts one that is wrong for the weather or the occasion on top (`TasteLearningTests`). When it shaped a look, the deck says so in one line under the note: "Picked because you often wear navy with grey".
- **Everything is changeable**: each look is shown as a composed outfit board (layers and top on the left, bottom on the right, shoes and accessories below). Tap a piece to **Keep** it or **Swap** it for the next best alternative, shuffle the rest around kept pieces, add a missing piece, or regenerate. "Wear today" logs the look (or drag the board right; left skips). With a small closet a tip says what to add for more variety.
- Each look comes with a short stylist note built from the scoring reasons: a weather lead, a colour story and a closer (3–4 per mood and register, plus the occasion's own). No lead or closer repeats within a deck of five (a restyled card avoids the others' phrases too), and "pops against" is kept for real contrasts, never a light-blue shirt with blue jeans (`StylistNoteTests`). On Apple Intelligence devices the on-device model can interpret free text and polish the note (`OnDeviceStylist`), always with a silent deterministic fallback.

### 3b. "On you" try-on (`Services/TryOn/`, docs/TRY-ON.md)

Each look in the deck has a **Pieces | On you (Beta)** toggle (Pieces by default): the outfit drawn on the user's own body. The body is their best full-length, front-facing photo among the photos the scan matched to them by face (Apple Vision body pose scores it; "Change photo" picks another, takes one, or uses a neutral model body generated once on the device and shown neck-down). Each piece's own image is split into parts (torso, sleeves; hips, legs) and mapped onto the skeleton, lit from the photo; what the old outfit leaves uncovered becomes skin or background, then a **differential-diffusion** pass with the studio's dreamshaper-8 model relights the garments (light only, never their colour) and repaints those areas. Face, hair and hands are never changed. A gate (FashionSigLIP similarity per piece on the body, colour shift, visibility, face untouched, how much was invented) falls back to the pieces when a try-on isn't faithful. Renders are cached by (body photo, items, item images), prepared in the background for the next looks under the studio queue's rules, full screen with pinch-zoom, and side by side with the pieces on the Duo's inner display. One switch, `TryOnAvailability`, gates the feature. No new model ships.

### 4. Outfit calendar

Kept outfits are logged to Core Data (`DatedOutfit` + `OutfitItem`) and appear in **My Outfits** as a week strip (or month grid) with each day's outfits as cutout collages (edit, delete, manual composer). The log feeds the stylist's recency signal, so you aren't offered what you wore yesterday.

The calendar also **fills itself** (`Services/Insights/WearLogSuggestions`): when the scan finds a photo of you from the last week (new photos are picked up automatically), its garments are matched to your closet and Home asks "Wore this on Tue? Camel coat + navy cardigan — **Log it**".

### 5. Morning outfit

`Services/Notifications/MorningLook`: an optional local notification at 7:30 (time editable in Profile) — "9° and cloudy — your camel coat look is ready" — with a picture of the look. The look is precomputed on the device by `OutfitEngine` when the app last ran and again in a `BGAppRefreshTask` before the alarm (with that morning's forecast), and tapping the notification opens Today's Outfit on exactly that look. Permission is asked only after the first saved outfit.

## Major features

- **Account: Sign in with Apple only** (`Services/AuthService`, `Services/Account/`, `Views/Account/`). No guest mode, no email/password, no Google. The subscription and the 50 free pieces belong to the account, on every device. The Apple user identifier is kept in the Keychain (`Account`); a deterministic UUID derived from it (`Account.uuid`, SHA-256, version-5 layout) is the StoreKit `appAccountToken` and names the account's CloudKit zone. Only the full name is requested (to greet the person), never the email. At launch and on every foreground `getCredentialState` runs (also `credentialRevokedNotification`): revoked or not found signs out. Everything on the phone stays keyed by `user.id`, the account's profile id: on a phone that had an earlier on-device profile (builds without sign-in), the first account to sign in adopts it, so the closet, outfits, calendar and face data stay. That account re-uploads the wardrobe to its own zone once. Sign out keeps the data on the phone. **Delete Account** (Profile › Account) deletes local data and the account's iCloud zone and forgets the account. Apple token revocation needs a server, so the delete screen shows the Settings steps instead (founder decision in `docs/LAUNCH-READINESS.md`). Rare case: if the Apple ID used for Sign in with Apple differs from the iPhone's iCloud account, the wardrobe is backed up in that iCloud account, in the SIWA account's own zone. It restores on another iPhone only if that phone uses the same iCloud account. Two Apple IDs sharing one iCloud never mix wardrobes.
- **Onboarding**: welcome & privacy → Sign in with Apple → three-frame selfie (skippable: without one there is no Find yourself or scan screen, and Home offers "Take a selfie to find your outfit photos" and "Try with sample photos") → photo permission (Limited access explained, "Select more photos", whatever is shared still gets scanned) → Find yourself → a live scan screen ("11 items found in 23 photos") that hands over to Home at 8 items or 90 s while the scan continues in the banner. **"Try with sample photos"** on the welcome screen (and on an empty closet) runs the same pipeline on bundled CC0 photos (`StyleMate/Resources/SamplePhotos`, credits in `SAMPLE-PHOTO-CREDITS.md` and in Profile); sample clothes are labelled and removable in one tap. No coach-mark tour auto-starts (replay it from Profile).
- **Closet-ready notifications** (`Services/Notifications/ClosetReadyNotifier`): during the first library scan, with StyleMate in the background, "Your closet is ready: N pieces" at 10, 25 and 50 pieces, once each, and only if notifications are already allowed (it never asks).
- **Home**: greeting, weather, a "Today's look" card (the stylist's pick for today's weather as a small outfit board, with Wear today / More options; once you've logged a look it shows what you're wearing; until the closet can make a whole outfit — a top and a bottom or a dress, plus shoes — an **"Almost ready"** card says what's missing instead, "Add 1 more bottom and shoes to get outfits", `OutfitReadiness`), "Style me" with occasion and mood chips, wardrobe summary, scan progress, "Is this you?" review banner, last-scan results. Plus the first-run / habit cards (`HomeFirstRunCards`: sample clothes, "We weren't sure about 1 item — tap to add", "Wore this on Tue? — Log it", the morning-outfit ask).
- **My Wardrobe**: a closet of cutout tiles on shelves (tops & layers, bottoms, dresses, footwear, bags, accessories, jewelry); filter by shelf, colour and pattern; sort by newest, colour or how often you wear it; search; tap to zoom into an item page (attributes, wear history, edit, "Remove from wardrobe"); multi-select to remove misidentified items. Add via the + button: Camera / Gallery / Auto-scan.
- **My Outfits**: week strip or month calendar of logged outfits.
- **Wardrobe insights** (Profile → Your style; `Views/Insights/`, `Services/Insights/WardrobeInsights`): the share of the closet worn in the last 60 days, most worn, not worn in 60 days (only claimed once the outfit log goes back that far; "not worn yet" before), the newest pieces, colour balance (with the share of neutrals), pieces per shelf with a note when tops outnumber bottoms 3 : 1, and near-duplicates ("You have 9 black tees"). Computed on the phone from the closet and the calendar. Items have no price field, so there is no cost-per-wear. On the Duo's inner display it is two `FoldAwareSplit` columns. **Available to everyone today**; `InsightsAvailability.isAvailable` is the single switch that makes it a Premium perk (one line; Profile then shows a Premium badge that opens the paywall).
- **Large displays (iPhone Duo inner display)**: two panes via `FoldAwareSplit` (split exactly on the fold, open flat as well as half-open, so no control sits on the crease): the closet grid beside the item page; Today's Outfit's board beside the note, a Keep / Swap list and all five options; Home, Outfits and Profile in two columns; the add sheet's rows either side of the fold when it is centred on it. The cover display and other iPhones keep the phone layout.
- **Dark mode**: every screen follows the system appearance. Product shots stay on a dimmed light "studio print" (closet tiles, outfit boards, item page), so dark garments stay visible; the mint accent carries dark text and no glow; cards keep a subtle rim instead of a shadow. The app icon has dark and tinted variants. CI's UI tests rerun the end-to-end flows in dark appearance (`-uiTestDarkMode`, DEBUG only) and save those screenshots with a `-dark` suffix.
- **Profile**: about you (name, gender/age — optional), StyleMate Premium (plan, renewal date, Manage subscription, Restore Purchases), preferred styles, your style (Wardrobe insights, Reset style learning), morning-outfit notification + time, cloud sync status, recognition tools, privacy policy, sample-clothes removal, tour replay, About (acknowledgements, diagnostics, version), data deletion (wardrobe, outfit log, style learning, selfie/face data, scan state, the account's iCloud zone), Account (Sign Out, Delete Account).
- **About → Diagnostics** (`Views/Diagnostics/`, `Services/Diagnostics/`): phone facts (model, iOS, temperature, Low Power Mode, battery, memory, storage, Core ML compute devices incl. the Neural Engine), a **benchmark** on the 6 bundled sample photos (garment extraction cold + warm, face detect + embed, one studio render; 3 rounds, medians; peak memory; which device Core ML's compute plan puts each bundled model on), **live stats** from real use (`PerfLog`: rolling s/photo, photos checked, items found, studio renders kept / rejected / average time, kept in `Application Support/Diagnostics/`), an **iPhone Duo hinge check** (live angle, posture and fold; guided flat / ~90° / closed readings vs target), and a plain-text **Share report**. Nothing is sent anywhere unless the user shares the report. `PerfLog` also emits Instruments signposts (Points of Interest).
- **About → Acknowledgements**: version, privacy policy, and every licence / attribution text in `StyleMate/Resources/Acknowledgements/` (one `ACK-<order>-<slug>.md` per component, listed automatically: Marqo-FashionSigLIP + SigLIP, Stable Diffusion 1.5 / DreamShaper 8 with the OpenRAIL-M use restrictions, Apple ml-stable-diffusion, YuNet and SFace, MET Norway and Open-Meteo weather data, and, until the garment segmenters are replaced, the FASHN parser and SegFormer Fashionpedia labeler (NVIDIA SegFormer licence text) and the Fashionpedia dataset (CC BY 4.0), as `ACK-90`–`ACK-92`).
- **StyleMate Premium (subscription)**: every feature is free for the first **50 wardrobe items per account** (counted across all the account's devices; sample-photo items don't count); Premium, a StoreKit 2 auto-renewable subscription (group "StyleMate Premium": `com.stylemates.StyleMate.premium.annual` with a 1-week free trial, and `…premium.monthly`; prices always from StoreKit), makes the wardrobe unlimited. At the limit, camera / gallery adds open the paywall, a review batch that would cross 50 adds what fits and saves the rest, and the background scan keeps finding pieces but stores them **locked** (`WardrobeViewModel.lockedItems`: synced with the account, not in the closet or outfits) behind a "23 more pieces found — Unlock with Premium" card on Home and in the closet. Subscribing unlocks them at once; removing items lets the oldest locked ones fill the free slots; if Premium lapses nothing already in the closet is removed, only new additions past 50 wait. The paywall (`Views/Subscription/PaywallView`) shows the user's own cutouts, StoreKit prices / periods / trial (only when eligible), the auto-renew terms, Restore Purchases, Terms of Use (Apple's standard EULA) and the privacy policy; on the Duo's inner display it is a `FoldAwareSplit` (closet | plans). The entitlement comes from `Transaction.currentEntitlements` + the group's renewal status (trial, billing grace period, billing retry, refunds, Ask to Buy), with `Transaction.updates` heard from launch and a cached copy for offline / background launches. Profile shows the plan and renewal date with Manage Subscription and Restore. No server, no receipts sent anywhere, no analytics. App Store Connect setup: `docs/TESTFLIGHT.md` §6.
- **The account-level free tier** (`Services/Subscription/AccountAllowance`): every item carries synced bookkeeping (`createdAt`, `tier` grandfathered / premium / free, `isSample`; `isLocked` for waiting pieces). Waiting pieces live in `WardrobeViewModel.lockedItems` and sync like every item, so every device agrees. After each sync merge (and on launch, entitlement changes and removals) every device runs the same pure rule:
  - With Premium, everything unlocks and free pieces become `premium`, so a lapse removes nothing.
  - Without Premium, grandfathered (items from before this build) and premium pieces always stay. The free-tier and waiting pieces are ordered by `createdAt`, then id, and the oldest that fit in `50 − kept` are unlocked, the rest locked.
  - The result depends only on the set of items, not on merge order. Offline adds go up to the phone's own view of the limit. If a sync then has to lock pieces the phone was showing, Home says "3 pieces moved to Premium because your other iPhone added items".
- **Which purchases count for an account** (`PremiumStatus.belongs`):
  - Its own `appAccountToken`: counts.
  - No token (offer codes, App Store page purchases, builds before accounts): counts.
  - Family Sharing: counts.
  - Another StyleMate account's token: doesn't count. Profile then says "This Apple Account's subscription belongs to another StyleMate account". StoreKit transactions follow the App Store account, so a second SIWA account on the same App Store account can't use the first one's subscription.
- **Privacy policy**: `docs/privacy.html`, published at https://pravegm.github.io/StyleMate/privacy.html (GitHub Pages from `docs/`).
- **CloudKit sync**: private database, one zone per StyleMate account (`Wardrobe-<account uuid>`), per-item records with image assets. Incremental: a persisted server change token, metadata-only pulls, image assets fetched lazily off the main thread. The phone is the source of truth — local changes are queued in a per-user ledger (pending uploads + tombstones) and retried until iCloud confirms them, local edits win, and a local item is only removed when the server reports its deletion. Pulls restore every record in the zone whatever profile id uploaded it (the private database is the iCloud account's own), so a reinstall or a new iPhone — which gets a new local profile id — restores the whole wardrobe; "Delete Everything" blocks all pulls until iCloud confirms the zone deletion.

## Architecture

- **Language / UI**: Swift, SwiftUI (UIKit interop for `ImagePicker` and camera capture). MVVM.
- **On-device ML (Core ML)**: YuNet face detector, SFace face embedder, GarmentNet garment segmenter (DINOv2 + StyleMate's Fashionpedia head), Marqo-FashionSigLIP encoder, and the studio-render diffusion model (DreamShaper 8 img2img: text encoder, UNet, VAE encoder/decoder). Plus Apple Vision (person-instance masks, human detection) and, where available, Apple's on-device Foundation Models.
- **Try-on files**: the chosen body photo and finished try-ons under `Application Support/TryOn/` (local only, excluded from backup).
- **Local persistence**: `UserDefaults` (wardrobe items per user, sync ledger), wardrobe images / thumbnails / outfit-history snapshots under `Application Support/wardrobe_images/`, the selfie under `Application Support/Selfie/`, the face gallery under `Application Support/FaceReference/` (with per-entry provenance: selfie / confirmed / learned), item embeddings under `Application Support/WardrobeEmbeddings/items.bin` (compact binary, written off the main thread, debounced), studio renders' bookkeeping under `Application Support/Studio/`, Core Data (outfit history, scoped per user; a damaged store is moved to `Application Support/CoreDataBackups/` instead of crashing). Personal files are excluded from iCloud Backup and use `completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication` file protection (`LocalStore`); older builds' `Documents/` files are moved there automatically.
- **Cloud**: CloudKit private DB only (your own iCloud). No third-party AI services.
- **Weather**: met.no primary with Open-Meteo fallback (no key).

## Permissions

Declared in `Info.plist`: **Photos** (wardrobe building and scan; `PHPhotoLibraryPreventAutomaticLimitedAccessAlert`, since the app offers "Select more photos" itself), **Camera** (selfie and item capture), **Location** (optional, weather only: When In Use, approximate by default via `NSLocationDefaultAccuracyReduced`, kilometre accuracy; the weather request carries it rounded to 2 decimals); **Notifications** are requested at run time (morning outfit, after the first saved outfit). `UIBackgroundModes` `processing` + `fetch`, and `BGTaskSchedulerPermittedIdentifiers` = `com.stylemates.StyleMate.photoscan` (overnight scan) and `com.stylemates.StyleMate.morninglook` (morning-look refresh). Entitlements: iCloud / CloudKit and Sign in with Apple (`com.apple.developer.applesignin`); the Premium subscription uses StoreKit, which needs no entitlement (In-App Purchase is on for every App ID). Core ML runs on CPU + Neural Engine only (no GPU, which iOS forbids in the background), and Core Image renders use a software context whenever the app isn't active (`CoreImageContext`); a render that still comes back empty leaves the photo to be scanned again.

## Setup

Nobody needs a local Mac build to work on StyleMate: **every branch push runs `.github/workflows/ci.yml`** on GitHub's Apple-silicon `xcode-27` runner. It regenerates all models into the Actions cache, builds the app, and runs the simulator tests (including end-to-end garment extraction on CC0 street photos in `StyleMateTests/Fixtures/`, whose cutouts are uploaded in the `ci-report` artifact).

**Wardrobe benchmark**: `WardrobeBenchmarkTests` scores extraction against hand labels (`StyleMateTests/Fixtures/labels/<photo>.json`: every garment's kind, accepted products and colours, pattern, sleeve and box). Predictions are matched to labels by box overlap; precision / recall, category, product and colour accuracy, pattern errors, fragment rate, seconds per photo and the duplicate-threshold check go to `benchmark.json` and `duplicates.json` in the report. The test fails if a headline metric drops more than 3 points below `labels/baseline.json`; update that file when a change is a real improvement. The try-on tests composite 5 outfits on 4 CC0 bodies, gate them and run one harmonised render (`tryon/`: sheets, `report.json`, `timing.txt`). The studio tests save every render candidate with its scores (`studio_pipeline/`) and compare a Core ML render with a diffusers reference of the same request. **Studio coverage**: the `studio` CI job (three runners, one shard each; only when the commit message says `[studio]` or `[full]`, or a dispatch sets `studio`) renders fixed cutouts of shoes (white and black sneakers, patent boots, oxfords, boat shoes), hats (a cap, a wide-brim hat), full-length trousers (chinos, black trousers, leggings, joggers, skinny jeans) and a light-grey coat (`StudioCoverageTests`, `StyleMateTests/Fixtures/studio/studio_manifest.json`), and uploads every template, candidate, verdict and score as `studio-coverage-<shard>`.

**End-to-end UI tests**: the `ui-tests` CI job drives the real app in the simulator with XCUITest (`StyleMateUITests/`). It covers the closet, "Style Me" (mood → 5 options → swap a piece → save → the look appears on today's date in the calendar), manual outfit logging, and a gallery import through the system photo picker into on-device extraction. DEBUG-only launch arguments (`-uiTestResetState`, `-uiTestDemoUser` (a fake signed-in account), `-uiTestFakeAppleSignIn` ("Continue as test user" under the real Sign in with Apple button: the CI simulator can't do real SIWA), `-uiTestSkipOnboarding`, `-uiTestSeedWardrobe`, in `StyleMate/Debug/DebugLaunchSupport.swift`) sign in a demo user and seed a wardrobe. `TasteInsightsE2ETests` (light and dark) checks the deck's "Picked because…" line and the "Not my style / Not today" choice after a skip (`-uiTestSeedTaste` seeds a learned profile, DEBUG only), Home's "Almost ready" card on a two-tee closet, and Wardrobe insights; `DuoScreensTests.test03_DuoTasteAndInsights` shoots the deck and the insights screen on the Duo's displays. Screenshots of every step are uploaded as the `e2e-screens` artifact. The simulator build is ad-hoc signed so CloudKit's entitlements survive.

**Subscriptions in tests**: `StyleMate.storekit` (repo root) defines the two Premium plans for StoreKit Test; the shared scheme (`StyleMate.xcodeproj/xcshareddata/xcschemes/StyleMate.xcscheme`) selects it for Run and Test, and the tests also open it with `SKTestSession`. `SubscriptionTests` covers the 50-item gate, locked items, the entitlement rules and a real StoreKit Test purchase; `SubscriptionE2ETests` (light and dark) seeds a closet at 50 with 23 locked pieces (`-uiTestSeedWardrobeCount 50 -uiTestSeedLocked 23`, DEBUG only), opens the paywall, buys the annual plan and checks the pieces unlock, and checks that a gallery add at the limit opens the paywall. `DuoScreensTests.test02_DuoPaywall` shoots the paywall on the Duo's cover, flat and half-open displays.

To build on a Mac (Xcode 27+, iOS 18.4+ device or simulator):

1. `git clone https://github.com/pravegm/StyleMate.git`
2. Generate the git-ignored models (Python 3.11):
   ```bash
   bash scripts/build_face_models.sh       # -> StyleMate/FaceModels/FaceDetectYuNet + FaceEmbedSFace .mlpackage
   gh run download <run_id in scripts/garment/model.json> -n garment-head -D scripts/garment/.artifacts
   bash scripts/build_wardrobe_models.sh   # -> StyleMate/ML/GarmentNet + FashionEncoder .mlpackage + FashionVocabulary.json
   bash scripts/build_studio_models.sh     # -> StyleMate/ML/Studio/StudioModels.bundle (macOS + Xcode, 30-90 min)
   ```
   YuNet takes BGR raw 0–255, SFace RGB raw 0–255 (see `FaceModelProfile`); `scripts/convert_face_models.py` and `convert_wardrobe_models.py` parity-check Core ML against the originals. `scripts/check_face_models.sh` is the licence gate CI and TestFlight run on the built app.
3. `open StyleMate.xcodeproj`, set your signing team, and run (a real device for the selfie camera).

### iPhone Duo

StyleMate runs natively full screen on the iPhone Duo's inner and cover displays (no `UIRequiresFullScreen`, all iPhone orientations; build with **Xcode 27.1+** so the binary carries the iOS 27.1 SDK, or the Duo letterboxes it). `StyleMate/Views/Components/DuoLayout.swift` publishes `\.duoLayout` (wide layout, posture — flat / book / tabletop / closed — hinge angle and the fold's position, from `onHingeChange` and the `.division` reserved region) and provides `FoldAwareSplit`, a two-pane container that splits at the crease whenever the inner display has one: half-open (the active fold) and open flat (the inactive fold, which the system still reports in place).

`.github/workflows/duo.yml` (every push to `feat/duo`, or **Actions → Duo → Run workflow**) downloads the iOS 27.1 runtime, builds for an iPhone Duo simulator and runs `StyleMateUITests/DuoScreensTests`: the test navigates on the cover display and calls `scripts/duo_bridge.py`, which moves the simulated hinge (`brew install artemnovichkov/tap/hinge`) and captures every screen on the cover, inner flat (180°) and inner half-open (110°, book pose) displays, and copies the app's own posture / crease log. Flat and half-open, the test also fails if any control's frame (its accessibility snapshot, clipped to the scroll views around it) crosses the fold band the app itself reports (the bridge reads the app's DEBUG `duo-log.txt`). (Seated / tabletop pose can only be checked on a device: the simulator shows the inner display sideways and refuses programmatic rotation.) Frames land in the `duo-screens` artifact with a `MANIFEST.txt`.

## Privacy

- Face matching, garment extraction, classification and outfit styling all run **on the device**. Selfies, face embeddings and photos never leave the phone — they are also kept out of iCloud Backup. Release builds log no user or photo identifiers (verbose logs are DEBUG-only, `dlog`).
- Apple's Foundation Models are used only through the on-device `SystemLanguageModel`.
- Wardrobe data syncs only to the user's own private CloudKit database, in a zone per StyleMate account. The account is Sign in with Apple (the Apple ID stays in the Keychain; only the name is requested). There is no StyleMate server.
- What the stylist learns from your choices (`TasteProfile`) stays on the iPhone: it is not synced, not in any report, fades over time and can be reset from Profile.
- Policy: `docs/privacy.html` (https://pravegm.github.io/StyleMate/privacy.html).
