# StyleMate

An iOS app that turns your camera roll into an organized digital wardrobe and styles you every day — **entirely on your iPhone**. No photo, face or wardrobe data is sent to any AI service.

StyleMate finds the photos of *you* in your library, cuts out every garment and accessory you're wearing, names it (category, product, colours, pattern, material, fit…), de-duplicates it against what you already own, and files it into your closet. When you want to get dressed, it builds five outfits for today from your own clothes — tuned to the weather, the occasion, your mood and what you've worn recently — and lets you log the look on a calendar.

## How it works

### 1. Finding you in your photos (on-device face matching)

- During onboarding you take a **three-frame selfie** (straight on, then a slight turn each way; `SelfieCameraService`). Each frame is checked on-device (one face, big enough, centred, lit, no sunglasses) before it's accepted.
- Then **Find yourself** (`Services/Identity/`, `FindYourselfView`) looks through up to ~1,500 recent photos, embeds every usable face, groups faces of the same person (greedy centroid clustering), ranks the groups by similarity to your selfie and asks "Is this you?" with one tap to confirm. The confirmed faces become your reference **gallery**, so the scan recognises you across angles, lighting and years.
- Faces are detected and 5-point-landmarked by the bundled **YuNet** detector (OpenCV Zoo, MIT; Core ML), aligned to the canonical ArcFace 112×112 template, and embedded with **SFace** (OpenCV Zoo, Apache-2.0; 128-d, flip-averaged). Large library photos get an extra pass over four overlapping tiles, so distant faces aren't missed. Every similarity bar lives in one profile (`FaceModelProfile`), calibrated on LFW; embeddings are tagged with the model version so a model change rebuilds the gallery. Both models are commercially usable; the earlier InsightFace SCRFD + AdaFace pair (non-commercial) is no longer built or shipped (`docs/MODEL-LICENSING.md`).
- Each library photo is matched by **cosine similarity** against the gallery with tiered confidence and a runner-up margin. Auto-adding needs the gallery's consensus (the mean of the two best-matching gallery faces), so one odd gallery entry can never pull a stranger in; review needs one good match:
  - **confident** → analysed straight away (in a multi-person photo only *your* body is isolated via a person-instance mask, so other people's clothes are never extracted); very confident matches that also match your **selfie** (and clearly beat anyone else in the shot) **teach the gallery** a new angle of your face — learned faces are capped at half the gallery and never displace the selfie or faces you confirmed;
  - **probably you** → queued for an **"Is this you?"** review (home banner → `FaceReviewView`); confirming teaches the gallery and extracts those outfits;
  - anyone else → skipped.
- The scan (`PhotoScanService` + `Services/Scan/`) starts with the **last 12 months**, with items appearing live ("All photos" is one tap away). The very first scan begins with a **"First 40" pass** (`FirstLook`): it ranks the last 90 days by Vision full-body person detection on small thumbnails (one person, whole body in view, a face to recognise; screenshots, documents and scenery drop out) and extracts the best ~40 first, so the closet fills within minutes; then it continues newest first. It skips screenshots, tiny images and panoramas, keeps only the best frame of a burst / same-moment group, pipelines loading and identity ahead of extraction, and paces itself by thermal state and Low Power Mode.
- It downloads iCloud-only originals when needed ("Optimize iPhone Storage"), retries photos that failed to load, and checkpoints progress to `Application Support/ScanProgress/` so it resumes exactly. It keeps going briefly when you leave the app, continues **overnight while charging** (`BGProcessingTask`), and picks up **new photos automatically** when you come back (PhotoKit persistent change history).

### 2. Building the wardrobe (on-device garment extraction — `Services/Wardrobe/`)

For each photo of you, `WardrobeExtractor`:

1. **Finds and crops the person** at a 2:3 portrait aspect so the garments fill the analysis grid.
2. **Parses** the crop with two Core ML segmentation models on a shared 384×576 grid:
   - **GarmentParser** — the FASHN human parser (SegFormer-B4): which pixels are clothing vs face/hair/arms/hands/legs/torso, and the coarse region (top, dress, skirt, pants, shoes, bag, hat, scarf, glasses, belt, jewelry). Skin never leaks into a garment.
   - **GarmentLabeler** — a Fashionpedia SegFormer-B3: the garment *type* per pixel, plus its hood / collar / lapel / sleeve *part* channels. `GarmentSegmenter` votes inside each region, which splits an open coat or jacket from the top underneath it, a black top from black jeans the parser merged into one "jumpsuit", and black boots from black trousers or tights. The part channels confirm hoods (no "hoodie" without one), sleeves and tailored lapels.
   - Grey padding past the photo's edge is masked out, so it never becomes garment. **Close-up passes** re-parse the head, each hand and the feet at full resolution to find what the full-body pass missed (sunglasses, a cap or turban, a watch, a bag, shoes).
3. **Cuts each item out** at full resolution (`GarmentCutout`): edge-aware mask upsampling guided by the photo, colour decontamination of soft edges (no halos), and a pyramid fill of small holes where a hand, arm, hair, strap or belt covered the fabric. Every cutout is trimmed and centred on a transparent square, and shown on a soft studio backdrop (`ItemCutoutTile`).
4. **Names it** (`FashionClassifier`): **Marqo-FashionSigLIP** (Apache-2.0) embeds the cutout and is compared zero-shot against precomputed text embeddings (`FashionVocabulary.json`) for product (and therefore category), pattern, material, fit, sleeve, neckline and length. Tops, layers and one-pieces are ranked across all three groups (a back-view coat the labeler called a dress becomes a coat); a top seen only between jacket halves or cut off by the frame is limited to everyday tops; rare prints need a clear margin over "Solid". Colours are *measured*: k-means (k=5, merged shades) over the fabric pixels in CIELAB after a damped white balance / exposure correction from the scene, named by hue family then lightness and chroma (so denim is Blue and a dusty-pink coat Pink, not Charcoal and Gray). On Apple Intelligence devices (iOS 27+), Apple's **on-device** foundation model (`SystemLanguageModel`, never Private Cloud Compute) adds a short details line and any clearly readable brand (`GarmentDescriber`).
5. **Scores the view** (size, sharpness, not cut off by the frame, little occlusion). Items too small or too poor to make a good wardrobe image are skipped — a better photo will catch them.
6. **Trust before volume** (`ItemTrust`): the scan only adds items it's sure about. Cutouts that split into several pieces (hair + lens "ties"), views with quality under 0.4, and small jewelry / ties / belts without a clear view are held back — Home shows "We weren't sure about 1 item — tap to add" — and start deselected in the manual review screen.

6. **Studio image** (`Services/Wardrobe/Studio/`): every item gets a lifelike catalog photo made **on the iPhone** by a Stable Diffusion 1.5-class model (dreamshaper-8, Core ML on the Neural Engine). Tops, layers and dresses are *generated* by image-to-image (0.65) from a **flat-lay silhouette filled with the garment's own fabric** (`StudioTemplate`: t-shirt, shirt, jacket, coat, tunic, dress or tank; trousers for bottoms cut off by the frame), which locks one whole garment, centred, in its real colour and texture. Text-to-image is only the fallback; without a template the model tends to draw a person wearing the garment. Trousers, shoes, bags and hats are *refined* from the cutout (image-to-image at ~0.5), which keeps their exact look. Anything cut off by the photo frame, or with occluded parts left unfilled, is generated. Several candidates are rendered in one batch and cut out of their white background. A candidate is rejected outright for a second object, a white panel, a body or mannequin, touching the frame, a "failed render" look (zero-shot), or too little FashionSigLIP similarity to your item (0.80 for a refine, 0.70 text-to-image, 0.55 template). The best of the rest wins; if none is left, the clean cutout stays. Renders run in the background (`StudioQueue`), always from the original cutout, and wait while a photo scan runs, the phone is hot, in Low Power Mode, low on battery or memory; they stop when the app is backgrounded. The item page can flip between **Studio** and **From your photo**. Timing: the CI simulator runs the UNet on its CPU (about 15 min for two candidates); on an iPhone's Neural Engine expect roughly a second per denoising step, so about 40–60 s per item (an estimate from Apple's ml-stable-diffusion benchmarks for SD 1.5, not yet measured on a device). The closet keeps showing the cutout until the render lands.

Then `DuplicateDetector` compares the item with the wardrobe using the visual embeddings (`ItemEmbeddingStore`) plus attribute gates. The same garment seen again in a **better** photo **upgrades** the wardrobe image instead of adding a duplicate.

There are three ways to add items, all through the same pipeline: **Auto-scan** of a date range, **Photo picker** (up to 15 photos, with "which one is you?" when several people are in a shot), and **Camera**. Picked photos are downsampled to 2048 px, and every extraction (manual or scan) goes through one shared gate (`ExtractionGate`, 1–2 at a time) so memory stays bounded.

### 3. "What should I wear today?" (on-device stylist — `Services/Outfits/`)

`OutfitEngine` builds **five distinct outfits** from your wardrobe:

- **Inputs**: today's weather (met.no, Open-Meteo fallback; city via `CoreLocation`), the occasion (`OutfitType`) or free text ("dinner date, nothing black"), your **mood** (`OutfitMood`: confident, cozy, relaxed, playful, polished, bold, minimal, romantic), your saved style preferences, and your **recent wear history** from the outfit calendar.
- **Rules + scoring**: slot-based construction (top + bottom or a one-piece, optional mid-layer and outerwear, shoes, 0–2 accessories), temperature bands and rain rules, formality fit per occasion, colour harmony on a colour wheel (neutral base + accents, tonal, analogous, complementary; at most one bold pattern), mood and preference fit, classic do-not-pairs, a recency penalty and a learned-taste term (below). A diversity pass keeps the five looks genuinely different.
- **It learns your taste, on the iPhone** (`TasteProfile`, `TasteStore`): "Wear today" teaches the look's pieces, its colour pairings and how dressy you went for that occasion (an outfit logged by hand in My Outfits, or a "Wore this on Tue?" you confirm, teaches the pieces and pairings too); Keep (lock) teaches the piece; Swap marks the swapped-out piece gently; Skip is a weak signal, with an optional "Not my style / Not today" right after it ("Not my style" teaches properly, "Not today" teaches nothing). The model is a few KB per local profile in UserDefaults: every value is capped, decays with a 45-day half-life and is pruned, it is never synced or sent anywhere, and **Profile → Your style → Reset style learning** clears it. In the scorer it is one bounded term (±2) next to the recency penalty, and it may only *promote* a look whose taste-free score is already within 1.2 of the best, so learning reorders good looks but never puts one that is wrong for the weather or the occasion on top (`TasteLearningTests`). When it shaped a look, the deck says so in one line under the note: "Picked because you often wear navy with grey".
- **Everything is changeable**: each look is shown as a composed outfit board (layers and top on the left, bottom on the right, shoes and accessories below). Tap a piece to **Keep** it or **Swap** it for the next best alternative, shuffle the rest around kept pieces, add a missing piece, or regenerate. "Wear today" logs the look (or drag the board right; left skips). With a small closet a tip says what to add for more variety.
- Each look comes with a short stylist note built from the scoring reasons: a weather lead, a colour story and a closer (3–4 per mood and register, plus the occasion's own). No lead or closer repeats within a deck of five (a restyled card avoids the others' phrases too), and "pops against" is kept for real contrasts, never a light-blue shirt with blue jeans (`StylistNoteTests`). On Apple Intelligence devices the on-device model can interpret free text and polish the note (`OnDeviceStylist`), always with a silent deterministic fallback.

### 4. Outfit calendar

Kept outfits are logged to Core Data (`DatedOutfit` + `OutfitItem`) and appear in **My Outfits** as a week strip (or month grid) with each day's outfits as cutout collages (edit, delete, manual composer). The log feeds the stylist's recency signal, so you aren't offered what you wore yesterday.

The calendar also **fills itself** (`Services/Insights/WearLogSuggestions`): when the scan finds a photo of you from the last week (new photos are picked up automatically), its garments are matched to your closet and Home asks "Wore this on Tue? Camel coat + navy cardigan — **Log it**".

### 5. Morning outfit

`Services/Notifications/MorningLook`: an optional local notification at 7:30 (time editable in Profile) — "9° and cloudy — your camel coat look is ready" — with a picture of the look. The look is precomputed on the device by `OutfitEngine` when the app last ran and again in a `BGAppRefreshTask` before the alarm (with that morning's forecast), and tapping the notification opens Today's Outfit on exactly that look. Permission is asked only after the first saved outfit.

## Major features

- **No account**: the app creates a local profile (a UUID) on first launch; there is no sign-in. Users of earlier builds that signed in with Apple or Google keep their user id, so their wardrobe stays.
- **Onboarding** (4 screens to scanning): welcome & privacy → three-frame selfie → photo permission (Limited access explained, "Select more photos", whatever is shared still gets scanned) → Find yourself → a live scan screen ("11 items found in 23 photos") that hands over to Home at 8 items or 90 s while the scan continues in the banner. **"Try with sample photos"** on the welcome screen (and on an empty closet) runs the same pipeline on bundled CC0 photos (`StyleMate/Resources/SamplePhotos`, credits in `SAMPLE-PHOTO-CREDITS.md` and in Profile); sample clothes are labelled and removable in one tap. No coach-mark tour auto-starts (replay it from Profile).
- **Home**: greeting, weather, a "Today's look" card (the stylist's pick for today's weather as a small outfit board, with Wear today / More options; once you've logged a look it shows what you're wearing; until the closet can make a whole outfit — a top and a bottom or a dress, plus shoes — an **"Almost ready"** card says what's missing instead, "Add 1 more bottom and shoes to get outfits", `OutfitReadiness`), "Style me" with occasion and mood chips, wardrobe summary, scan progress, "Is this you?" review banner, last-scan results. Plus the first-run / habit cards (`HomeFirstRunCards`: sample clothes, "We weren't sure about 1 item — tap to add", "Wore this on Tue? — Log it", the morning-outfit ask).
- **My Wardrobe**: a closet of cutout tiles on shelves (tops & layers, bottoms, dresses, footwear, bags, accessories, jewelry); filter by shelf, colour and pattern; sort by newest, colour or how often you wear it; search; tap to zoom into an item page (attributes, wear history, edit, "Remove from Wardrobe"); multi-select to remove misidentified items. Add via the + button: Camera / Gallery / Auto-scan.
- **My Outfits**: week strip or month calendar of logged outfits.
- **Wardrobe insights** (Profile → Your style; `Views/Insights/`, `Services/Insights/WardrobeInsights`): the share of the closet worn in the last 60 days, most worn, not worn in 60 days (only claimed once the outfit log goes back that far; "not worn yet" before), the newest pieces, colour balance (with the share of neutrals), pieces per shelf with a note when tops outnumber bottoms 3 : 1, and near-duplicates ("You have 9 black tees"). Computed on the phone from the closet and the calendar. Items have no price field, so there is no cost-per-wear. On the Duo's inner display it is two `FoldAwareSplit` columns. **Available to everyone today**; `InsightsAvailability.isAvailable` is the single switch that makes it a Premium perk (one line; Profile then shows a Premium badge that opens the paywall).
- **Large displays (iPhone Duo inner display)**: two panes via `FoldAwareSplit` (split exactly on the crease when half-open): the closet grid beside the item page; Today's Outfit's board beside the note, a Keep / Swap list and all five options; Home and Outfits in two columns. The cover display and other iPhones keep the phone layout.
- **Dark mode**: every screen follows the system appearance. Product shots stay on a dimmed light "studio print" (closet tiles, outfit boards, item page), so dark garments stay visible; the mint accent carries dark text and no glow; cards keep a subtle rim instead of a shadow. The app icon has dark and tinted variants. CI's UI tests rerun the end-to-end flows in dark appearance (`-uiTestDarkMode`, DEBUG only) and save those screenshots with a `-dark` suffix.
- **Profile**: about you (name, gender/age — optional), StyleMate Premium (plan, renewal date, Manage Subscription, Restore Purchases), preferred styles, your style (Wardrobe insights, Reset style learning), morning-outfit notification + time, cloud sync status, recognition tools, privacy policy, sample-clothes removal, tour replay, About (acknowledgements, diagnostics, version), data deletion (wardrobe, outfit log, style learning, selfie/face data, scan state, iCloud zone; then a fresh local profile).
- **About → Diagnostics** (`Views/Diagnostics/`, `Services/Diagnostics/`): phone facts (model, iOS, temperature, Low Power Mode, battery, memory, storage, Core ML compute devices incl. the Neural Engine), a **benchmark** on the 6 bundled sample photos (garment extraction cold + warm, face detect + embed, one studio render; 3 rounds, medians; peak memory; which device Core ML's compute plan puts each bundled model on), **live stats** from real use (`PerfLog`: rolling s/photo, photos checked, items found, studio renders kept / rejected / average time, kept in `Application Support/Diagnostics/`), an **iPhone Duo hinge check** (live angle, posture and fold; guided flat / ~90° / closed readings vs target), and a plain-text **Share report**. Nothing is sent anywhere unless the user shares the report. `PerfLog` also emits Instruments signposts (Points of Interest).
- **About → Acknowledgements**: version, privacy policy, and every licence / attribution text in `StyleMate/Resources/Acknowledgements/` (one `ACK-<order>-<slug>.md` per component, listed automatically: Marqo-FashionSigLIP + SigLIP, Stable Diffusion 1.5 / DreamShaper 8 with the OpenRAIL-M use restrictions, Apple ml-stable-diffusion, MET Norway and Open-Meteo weather data).
- **StyleMate Premium (subscription)**: every feature is free for the first **50 wardrobe items** (sample-photo items don't count); Premium, a StoreKit 2 auto-renewable subscription (group "StyleMate Premium": `com.stylemates.StyleMate.premium.annual` with a 1-week free trial, and `…premium.monthly`; prices always from StoreKit), makes the wardrobe unlimited. At the limit, camera / gallery adds open the paywall, a review batch that would cross 50 adds what fits and saves the rest, and the background scan keeps finding pieces but stores them **locked** (`Services/Subscription/LockedItemsStore`: on the phone only, not in the closet, outfits or iCloud) behind a "23 more pieces found — Unlock with Premium" card on Home and in the closet. Subscribing unlocks them at once; removing items lets the oldest locked ones fill the free slots; if Premium lapses nothing already in the closet is removed, only new additions past 50 wait. The paywall (`Views/Subscription/PaywallView`) shows the user's own cutouts, StoreKit prices / periods / trial (only when eligible), the auto-renew terms, Restore Purchases, Terms of Use (Apple's standard EULA) and the privacy policy; on the Duo's inner display it is a `FoldAwareSplit` (closet | plans). The entitlement comes from `Transaction.currentEntitlements` + the group's renewal status (trial, billing grace period, billing retry, refunds, Ask to Buy), with `Transaction.updates` heard from launch and a cached copy for offline / background launches. Profile shows the plan and renewal date with Manage Subscription and Restore. No server, no receipts sent anywhere, no analytics. App Store Connect setup: `docs/TESTFLIGHT.md` §6.
- **Privacy policy**: `docs/privacy.html`, published at https://pravegm.github.io/StyleMate/privacy.html (GitHub Pages from `docs/`).
- **CloudKit sync**: private database, custom `WardrobeZone`, per-item records with image assets. Incremental: a persisted server change token, metadata-only pulls, image assets fetched lazily off the main thread. The phone is the source of truth — local changes are queued in a per-user ledger (pending uploads + tombstones) and retried until iCloud confirms them, local edits win, and a local item is only removed when the server reports its deletion. Pulls restore every record in the zone whatever profile id uploaded it (the private database is the iCloud account's own), so a reinstall or a new iPhone — which gets a new local profile id — restores the whole wardrobe; "Delete Everything" blocks all pulls until iCloud confirms the zone deletion.

## Architecture

- **Language / UI**: Swift, SwiftUI (UIKit interop for `ImagePicker` and camera capture). MVVM.
- **On-device ML (Core ML)**: YuNet face detector, SFace face embedder, FASHN human parser, Fashionpedia garment labeler, Marqo-FashionSigLIP encoder, and the studio-render diffusion model (DreamShaper 8 img2img: text encoder, UNet, VAE encoder/decoder). Plus Apple Vision (person-instance masks, human detection) and, where available, Apple's on-device Foundation Models.
- **Local persistence**: `UserDefaults` (wardrobe items per user, sync ledger), wardrobe images / thumbnails / outfit-history snapshots under `Application Support/wardrobe_images/`, the selfie under `Application Support/Selfie/`, the face gallery under `Application Support/FaceReference/` (with per-entry provenance: selfie / confirmed / learned), item embeddings under `Application Support/WardrobeEmbeddings/items.bin` (compact binary, written off the main thread, debounced), studio renders' bookkeeping under `Application Support/Studio/`, Core Data (outfit history, scoped per user; a damaged store is moved to `Application Support/CoreDataBackups/` instead of crashing). Personal files are excluded from iCloud Backup and use `completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication` file protection (`LocalStore`); older builds' `Documents/` files are moved there automatically.
- **Cloud**: CloudKit private DB only (your own iCloud). No third-party AI services.
- **Weather**: met.no primary with Open-Meteo fallback (no key).

## Permissions

Declared in `Info.plist`: **Photos** (wardrobe building and scan; `PHPhotoLibraryPreventAutomaticLimitedAccessAlert`, since the app offers "Select more photos" itself), **Camera** (selfie and item capture), **Location** (optional, weather only); **Notifications** are requested at run time (morning outfit, after the first saved outfit). `UIBackgroundModes` `processing` + `fetch`, and `BGTaskSchedulerPermittedIdentifiers` = `com.stylemates.StyleMate.photoscan` (overnight scan) and `com.stylemates.StyleMate.morninglook` (morning-look refresh). Entitlements: iCloud / CloudKit only (no sign-in); the Premium subscription uses StoreKit, which needs no entitlement (In-App Purchase is on for every App ID). Core ML runs on CPU + Neural Engine only (no GPU, which iOS forbids in the background).

## Setup

Nobody needs a local Mac build to work on StyleMate: **every branch push runs `.github/workflows/ci.yml`** on GitHub's Apple-silicon `xcode-27` runner. It regenerates all models into the Actions cache, builds the app, and runs the simulator tests (including end-to-end garment extraction on CC0 street photos in `StyleMateTests/Fixtures/`, whose cutouts are uploaded in the `ci-report` artifact).

**Wardrobe benchmark**: `WardrobeBenchmarkTests` scores extraction against hand labels (`StyleMateTests/Fixtures/labels/<photo>.json`: every garment's kind, accepted products and colours, pattern, sleeve and box). Predictions are matched to labels by box overlap; precision / recall, category, product and colour accuracy, pattern errors, fragment rate, seconds per photo and the duplicate-threshold check go to `benchmark.json` and `duplicates.json` in the report. The test fails if a headline metric drops more than 3 points below `labels/baseline.json`; update that file when a change is a real improvement. The studio tests save every render candidate with its scores (`studio_pipeline/`) and compare a Core ML render with a diffusers reference of the same request.

**End-to-end UI tests**: the `ui-tests` CI job drives the real app in the simulator with XCUITest (`StyleMateUITests/`). It covers the closet, "Style Me" (mood → 5 options → swap a piece → save → the look appears on today's date in the calendar), manual outfit logging, and a gallery import through the system photo picker into on-device extraction. DEBUG-only launch arguments (`-uiTestResetState`, `-uiTestDemoUser`, `-uiTestSkipOnboarding`, `-uiTestSeedWardrobe`, in `StyleMate/Debug/DebugLaunchSupport.swift`) sign in a demo user and seed a wardrobe. `TasteInsightsE2ETests` (light and dark) checks the deck's "Picked because…" line and the "Not my style / Not today" choice after a skip (`-uiTestSeedTaste` seeds a learned profile, DEBUG only), Home's "Almost ready" card on a two-tee closet, and Wardrobe insights; `DuoScreensTests.test03_DuoTasteAndInsights` shoots the deck and the insights screen on the Duo's displays. Screenshots of every step are uploaded as the `e2e-screens` artifact. The simulator build is ad-hoc signed so CloudKit's entitlements survive.

**Subscriptions in tests**: `StyleMate.storekit` (repo root) defines the two Premium plans for StoreKit Test; the shared scheme (`StyleMate.xcodeproj/xcshareddata/xcschemes/StyleMate.xcscheme`) selects it for Run and Test, and the tests also open it with `SKTestSession`. `SubscriptionTests` covers the 50-item gate, locked items, the entitlement rules and a real StoreKit Test purchase; `SubscriptionE2ETests` (light and dark) seeds a closet at 50 with 23 locked pieces (`-uiTestSeedWardrobeCount 50 -uiTestSeedLocked 23`, DEBUG only), opens the paywall, buys the annual plan and checks the pieces unlock, and checks that a gallery add at the limit opens the paywall. `DuoScreensTests.test02_DuoPaywall` shoots the paywall on the Duo's cover, flat and half-open displays.

To build on a Mac (Xcode 27+, iOS 18.4+ device or simulator):

1. `git clone https://github.com/pravegm/StyleMate.git`
2. Generate the git-ignored models (Python 3.11):
   ```bash
   bash scripts/build_face_models.sh       # -> StyleMate/FaceModels/FaceDetectYuNet + FaceEmbedSFace .mlpackage
   bash scripts/build_wardrobe_models.sh   # -> StyleMate/ML/*.mlpackage + FashionVocabulary.json
   bash scripts/build_studio_models.sh     # -> StyleMate/ML/Studio/StudioModels.bundle (macOS + Xcode, 30-90 min)
   ```
   YuNet takes BGR raw 0–255, SFace RGB raw 0–255 (see `FaceModelProfile`); `scripts/convert_face_models.py` and `convert_wardrobe_models.py` parity-check Core ML against the originals. `scripts/check_face_models.sh` is the licence gate CI and TestFlight run on the built app.
3. `open StyleMate.xcodeproj`, set your signing team, and run (a real device for the selfie camera).

### iPhone Duo

StyleMate runs natively full screen on the iPhone Duo's inner and cover displays (no `UIRequiresFullScreen`, all iPhone orientations; build with **Xcode 27.1+** so the binary carries the iOS 27.1 SDK, or the Duo letterboxes it). `StyleMate/Views/Components/DuoLayout.swift` publishes `\.duoLayout` (wide layout, posture — flat / book / tabletop / closed — hinge angle and the fold's position, from `onHingeChange` and the `.division` reserved region) and provides `FoldAwareSplit`, a two-pane container that splits at the crease when the phone is half-open.

`.github/workflows/duo.yml` (every push to `feat/duo`, or **Actions → Duo → Run workflow**) downloads the iOS 27.1 runtime, builds for an iPhone Duo simulator and runs `StyleMateUITests/DuoScreensTests`: the test navigates on the cover display and calls `scripts/duo_bridge.py`, which moves the simulated hinge (`brew install artemnovichkov/tap/hinge`) and captures every screen on the cover, inner flat (180°) and inner half-open (110°, book pose) displays, and copies the app's own posture / crease log. (Seated / tabletop pose can only be checked on a device: the simulator shows the inner display sideways and refuses programmatic rotation.) Frames land in the `duo-screens` artifact with a `MANIFEST.txt`.

## Privacy

- Face matching, garment extraction, classification and outfit styling all run **on the device**. Selfies, face embeddings and photos never leave the phone — they are also kept out of iCloud Backup. Release builds log no user or photo identifiers (verbose logs are DEBUG-only, `dlog`).
- Apple's Foundation Models are used only through the on-device `SystemLanguageModel`.
- Wardrobe data syncs only to the user's own private CloudKit database. There is no account and no StyleMate server.
- What the stylist learns from your choices (`TasteProfile`) stays on the iPhone: it is not synced, not in any report, fades over time and can be reset from Profile.
- Policy: `docs/privacy.html` (https://pravegm.github.io/StyleMate/privacy.html).
